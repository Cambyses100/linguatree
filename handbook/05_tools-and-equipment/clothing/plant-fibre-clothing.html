Plant-Fibre Clothing

Object, function and scope

Wearable plant coverings form an equipment family: hanging grass panels, broader mantles and later wrapped or plaited variants. Their material and fastening requirements differ. Fine spun cloth, footwear and waterproof clothing are not acquired by making the first coarse screen.

The present base route is a flexible twined grass back panel. Its purpose is to reduce direct airflow over part of the back while leaving the arms free. Plant-Fibre Mats covers bedding and other retained sheets; its stiff willow-foundation pad should not simply be worn as a garment.

Materials and construction route

Select long, clean, flexible dry grass from Dry Plant Insulation. Reject fouled, moulding, sharp or rapidly crumbling material. Keep a separate garment lot; do not count the same bundles in bedding, loose stores and worn equipment. Local grass suitability remains a field trial, not a botanical guarantee.

Arrange small overlapping hanging bundles to the chosen coverage. Practise a short top-edge section before committing to the panel. Twining crosses two working elements between neighbouring bundles. Inspect migration, bundle retention and row spacing as the width develops.

In the first instance, paired lengths of the existing single nettle yarn form light retaining rows. Pairing them in twining does not ply them into certified cord. Inspect and rework weak joins; use separately tested Nettle Cordage for the supported top edge and shoulder attachment. Rounded margins and accessible loose fastenings matter more than neat decoration. Avoid a tight loop around the neck or ties that hinder breathing, arm movement or removal.

Account for grass, working yarn, header and fastenings before construction. Stiff splints, wool, imported fibres and unrecorded pelt panels do not belong to this base route. Bark and pelt clothing are alternatives with their own preparation and coverage limits; a mixed covering must allocate each part explicitly.

Construction and performance checks

Lift and bend the panel before wearing it. Inspect creeping rows, slipping bundles, harsh stalks and loose ends. Check walking, crouching and arm raising in a short dry trial. Rework a pressure point and repeat the movement rather than treating first assembly as acceptance.

Observe covered area and actual handling separately from warmth. A reduction in felt airflow is not a measured insulation rating. Wind penetration through gaps, loft, fit and the remaining exposed body all affect the result. Rain can wet and collapse the material; a dry fit check does not establish weather resistance.

Projectile use needs a separate snag and movement check. Do not assume that a panel harmless during walking will clear the thrower’s sweep. Remove it for wet processing or close fire work if it would be exposed to water, heat or sparks; secure it away from the working area.

First narrative specimen and allocation

Day 39 makes a small separate top-edge sample using 0.05 kg grass and 2 m single yarn. Day 40 makes grass-back-panel-a, about 0.5 by 0.6 m, from another 0.55 kg grass, 12 m single yarn in paired rows and 1.2 m tested nettle cord. These are authored consumption estimates, not an experimentally validated bill of materials or a universal yield.

The panel survives short dry walking, crouching and arm-raise checks after one rubbing end is trimmed. It covers part of the back with an open front. The bark waist covering, rabbit pelvic pad and separate fox shoulder panel remain. The new Day 38 rabbit pelt is not yet incorporated. No head covering, footwear, waterproof cape or whole-core winter outfit is established.

Labour, maintenance and further variants

Day 36 gathers and sorts the grass. Day 39 tests the structure at small scale. Day 40 spends three active hours on construction and fitting, in addition to supplies and other inspections. Harvesting and sorting are not removed by the apparent simplicity of twining.

Air under cover and inspect after use. Repair creeping rows; replace crumbling or wet bundles with newly acquired material. Worn ties consume stock. Keep garments away from sparks and the separate firing area. Damp or compressed material cannot be restored to the usable dry inventory merely by renaming it.

A broader core mantle needs additional grass, fastening stock, construction time and front/side fit checks. Added insulation must be retained without blocking movement or encouraging chafing. A second panel cannot be counted until those materials and work exist. Fine textiles depend on a different supply and construction chain.

Evidence and limits

R23 supports the structure of twining. R26 records later museum examples and the disputed cape interpretation of Ötzi’s grass mat. Neither demonstrates this garment at 40,000 BP in Iberia, specifies the local grass or validates thermal performance. The present back-panel design and short-term result are authored physical trials. Replication must test wear, damp recovery, fastening safety and useful coverage before presenting a dependable field design.

Use through Day 45

The back panel is worn briefly on dry gathering/walking portions. Day 41’s brush snag is freed without a counted tear or new allocation. It is taken off for wet bank work, casting and frame construction so the fringe cannot catch. There is still no wet-weather, prolonged-wear or casting-clearance result, no front panel, head covering or footwear. The rabbit pelt D38 remains unassigned.

Separate front panel and use, Days 46–50

Days 46 and 48 each allocate 9 g ready fibre to about 7 m uneven single yarn after rejection within the portion. Day 49 uses 12 m single yarn, 0.5 kg newly gathered long grass and 1.2 m inspected light cord for a roughly 0.45 by 0.6 m front panel. The supported header sits below the collarbones, with loose accessible shoulder/side adjustment and no tight neck loop. The existing back panel remains a separate object. These are author allocation estimates, not experimentally validated yields.

Lift/bend checks and a short dry walk, crouch and reach expose a rubbing stem and migrating row; adjustment precedes repeat fitting. Together the two pieces screen most of the front/back core, leaving side gaps, upper chest and limbs exposed. Day 50’s dry gathering walk reveals header creep which is corrected with the existing tie. There is still no damp-weather, insulation-rating or casting-clearance result. Remove both pieces for water, fire and projectile work and keep them protected when off. The stiff willow bed pad remains bedding. The later pelt crown piece is separately allocated in Fur-On Pelt Clothing.

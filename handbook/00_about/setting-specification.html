Setting Specification

Purpose and canonical constraints

The project explores the physical and organisational dependencies of civilisation through a remembered technical handbook and one traveller’s experience. The narrative records one contingent path. The handbook compares paths, including inventions that could become useful earlier under different conditions.

The traveller arrives alone and naked in a fictional low-elevation valley of northern Iberia, approximately 40,000 calibrated years before 1950. The date is deliberately approximate. Regional human coexistence is plausible but does not prove a particular local encounter; this valley and its inhabitants are setting choices. See World and Research Register.

Arrival is in early autumn, during nut harvest. Sheltered woodland patches alternate with open slopes. The first month contains cool nights, wet weather and one light local frost, rather than sustained winter freezing. These conditions are authored for this sequence, not a reconstructed daily weather record.

The traveller

He does not age, but he can suffer exposure, disease, injury, exhaustion and death. Healing is ordinary unless a later explicit setting decision changes it. Long life does not guarantee psychological endurance or uninterrupted leadership.

Uploaded memory contains the technical and scientific knowledge available in his departure civilisation. It does not provide contemporary observations of this valley, unrecorded languages, guaranteed experimental outcomes, or solutions his civilisation never achieved.

Before departure he receives practical training in flaking, friction fire, basic fibre work, shelter and species identification. He has rehearsed primitive methods under controlled conditions. He has less field experience than local people and lacks their environmental knowledge. Training supports competence, not instant mastery.

Transit rule

Only the traveller’s living body, with its normal incorporated tissues and physiological contents, can pass. Clothing, loose seeds, concealed cargo, tools, implants and deliberately packaged supplies cannot pass, irrespective of whether they are organic or inorganic. This is a fictional transit constraint; it replaces the earlier shorthand about inorganic matter. It is not a claim about real biology or physics.

First-month resource assumptions

The landing valley has a productive, accessible hazel patch and further patches identified over the month. This is the principal food assumption supporting the opening. Harvest returns must still be recorded, patches can be depleted, and weather and territorial access can interrupt collection.

Willow, nettle, pine, oak and some birch occur in suitable local patches as setting assumptions. A gravel bar provides knappable stone, and a small bank exposure provides clay for trials. Finding a plant or clay deposit does not establish that it works for every proposed use.

Rabbit and small rodent captures are intermittent supplements. Freshwater crayfish occurrence is unresolved and is not used to support the revised food budget. Freshwater mussels are observed but not relied upon for meals. Dangerous carnivores may be present; fire and weapons do not guarantee deterrence.

The first month’s detailed food, fuel, water and object changes are in First Month Ledger. Measured-looking quantities are author estimates, not measurements by a naked traveller without a balance.

Local people

The first encountered group is Neanderthal. Eleven people are seen on one visit; this is not necessarily the group’s total population. Their language, kinship, leadership, resource rights and wider contacts are initially unknown.

They are skilled makers and users of local tools. This fictional group knows plant cordage, hide work, fire management and hafting, including stone-pointed spears. Day 25 shows a knapped stone point bound into a wooden shaft; the traveller still carries his simpler wooden-pointed spear. Both designs are compatible with Neanderthal weapon evidence (R21). Choosing a composite weapon here does not make every wooden spear inferior or establish this design at a real northern Iberian site. These choices are compatible with evidence for Neanderthal technological sophistication, without asserting that every group shared one uniform toolkit.

The traveller may contribute unfamiliar methods or a useful comparison. The group may teach him materials, technique and local judgement. Interest is not agreement, a gift is not a contract, and no garment or device guarantees respect.

Decisions intentionally left open

  • Timeline mechanics: whether intervention changes the departure timeline or produces another branch. Do not claim either as established fact in daily entries.
  • Asteroid engineering: exact object, warning interval and why available deflection capacity is inadequate. The premise currently retains centuries of warning; the scale and institutional constraints require later specification.
  • Regional chronology: a real archaeological location and narrower arrival date would require another evidence pass.
  • Later travel and biological change: no domesticated crops, animals or imported resources appear until their acquisition, selection or movement has been described.

These open decisions do not prevent revising the first month. Changes to them must be recorded here and in Revision Record.

First contact and the proposed thrower

Smoke and distant firelight are noticed on Day 19; observation follows on Day 21, route planning on Day 24, and the first approach is rebuffed on Day 25. One warning incident belongs to these individuals and circumstances, not a claim that Neanderthals were uniformly hostile. The traveller stays away on Day 26; no gifts, communal food or shared instruction are secured in the first month.

This group has competent spear users as well as cordage and hafting expertise. An atlatl is chosen as a possible useful comparison on Day 26. Its presence in their toolkit is unknown, and the traveller’s own assembled example is untested at Day 28. Much later direct European evidence (R16) supports the choice of a potentially unfamiliar device without proving ancient absence. Effective hand throwing (R17) prevents automatic technological superiority. Future contact requires successful practical tests and a willing audience.

Day 20’s unidentified movement near camp and Day 22’s single wolf sighting are modest danger events, with retreat and maintenance costs. They introduce no injuries, predator kills or new resources. Agelessness gives no immunity to a bite, spear thrust, infection or exposure.

Moon rhythm and reflection cutoffs — 4 October 2026

Arrival is near a new moon. Lunar Calendar uses a fictional Day 0 new-moon epoch and a mean 29.53-day cycle for author bookkeeping. New/full moon reviews occur roughly fifteen days apart, beginning with Days 15 and 30, then Days 44 and 59. This is a setting choice and approximate rhythm, not a historical ephemeris.

Reflections reviews only the completed milestone day’s knowledge and inventory. Continuous days and the existing four-week planning blocks remain; no Survival event or work budget is moved to make a rounded month length. New moon supplies no bright lunar disk; actual sky visibility still depends on weather and terrain.

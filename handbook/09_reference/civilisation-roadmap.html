Civilisation Roadmap

The dependency problem

The endpoint is a society capable of building, operating and replacing space systems, then designing a defence mission appropriate to the threatening object. One remembered design is insufficient: industry needs reproducible materials, energy, measurement, supply chains, institutions and skilled people.

This is a research roadmap, not a claim that every listed historical step is necessary. Routes should be compared by time, labour, material access, failure cost and long-term maintenance.

Food production does not have to mean immediate agriculture. Productive fisheries, managed wild resources and storage may support some specialisation first. Domestication needs suitable organisms, generations and incentives; the protagonist cannot summon modern crops.

Shortcuts worth investigating

Day 40 Technology and Winter Audit reviews the current assemblage against archaeological parallels. The immediate bottleneck is seasonal resilience: an enclosed dry sleeping space, useful insulation, food reserves and protected fuel take precedence over adding experimental devices. Month Two Outline carries that priority into the next recorded work.

  • Measurement and experimental records before metallurgy: cheap concepts can prevent expensive trial-and-error.
  • Wooden spindles before ceramic whorls: an alternative material removes a firing dependency.
  • Water treatment before pottery: bark vessels and heated stones offer a tested alternative route.
  • Mechanical hafting before glue: geometry and binding can secure some tools; adhesive improves particular joints.
  • Community instruction before complex machines: parallel expertise may outperform one exceptional maker.
  • Starting with the most useful local metal route rather than compulsory bronze-first progression.
  • Delaying technologies that are physically possible but have poor returns at the current population or mobility.

For each proposed shortcut, write its actual requirements and a counterexample where it would fail. Avoid labelling an entire historical development a wrong turn without assessing the constraints faced by its inventors.

The space branch cannot be made concrete until the asteroid specification is settled. R12 in Research Register supplies a real deflection demonstration, not the requirements for this fictional threat. First specify what departure humanity could already build and why that capacity was inadequate; otherwise the mission’s endpoint may contradict its opening.

How to assess an earlier innovation

Use a short comparison record: the problem, two routes, every input, required precision, available labour, time to a first useful result, maintenance, likely failure and who benefits. Then explain what remembered knowledge changes.

Knowledge can suggest a material substitution, remove an unnecessary intermediate step, guide an experiment or avoid a known failure. It cannot create a missing organism, local ore, energy source or cooperative workforce. Earlier feasibility and worthwhile adoption are different questions.

For example, the wooden spindle removes successful firing from one route. It may still be a poor investment if fibre preparation is the bottleneck or if a mobile group needs little fine yarn. A machine becomes a shortcut only when its entire process improves the relevant task.

A claimed historical wrong turn needs the same comparison under the resources, institutions and incentives of the people who chose it. The protagonist’s hindsight alone is not evidence that their choice was irrational.

Institutional dependencies

Communication and Exchange, Teaching and Knowledge Preservation, Measurement and Records and Sanitation and Camp Hygiene begin early. Larger production later needs resource agreements, dispute resolution, responsibility for shared equipment and succession.

The traveller’s mission succeeds only when capabilities survive his absence. Community production is verified separately from his own demonstrations.

Narrative scale

Days 1–28 cover immediate survival and first contact. The approved second-month outline follows independent seasonal preparation, practical testing and limited contact. Cooperation can develop later when repeated willing interaction and useful results justify it. Later parts should follow projects, migrations, generations and industrial programmes. Detail follows the dependency being tested, not a fixed calendar format.

Wooden Mallet and Wooden Splitting Wedge begin a limited route to useful wooden blanks; Plant-Fibre Footwear has a narrow test rather than a solved winter outfit. Day 55’s observer interest is an opening for future learning, with no cooperation yet demonstrated. The next scale change should follow a technical project and its adoption, keeping Teaching and Knowledge Preservation central. Remembered knowledge becomes a civilisation’s capability only when other people can choose, reproduce, maintain and extend useful work.

Important events and limits

  • Day 3: first ignition; the fire kit needs continued maintenance. Stored heat is not permanent access to fire.
  • Days 4–5: first vessel heat trial fails, then repair permits recorded boiling. Day 9 wear interrupts treatment; Day 10 repairs restore it.
  • Day 7: the awl works. A needle attempt fails; a usable eyed needle follows on Day 12.
  • Days 9–10: the first rabbit pelt becomes a small carrying sling; the load test is under one kilogram.
  • Days 11–16: a small pot is shaped, dried, fired, cooled and rejected for leakage. It remains a non-food sample; no ceramic cooking asset is counted.
  • Days 4–20: the nettle batch is retted, processed in two halves, and partly made into cord. The first processing date is Day 16; elapsed time is twelve days, not a universal recipe.
  • Days 13–14 and 24: small hides become a pelvic pad and narrow shoulder panel. They do not amount to a winter coat.
  • Day 21: a wooden spindle bypasses ceramic success. A few metres of single yarn do not demonstrate a sustained productivity improvement.
  • Day 23: the damaged snare is removed. A limited fox meat lot is cooked and eaten, with additional work and fuel; its damaged pelt becomes a limited panel on Day 24.
  • Day 25: the first approach is rebuffed. Samples and the first spindle are abandoned during retreat, not accepted gifts. Day 26 records self-repair and planning, with no fibre exchange or shared teaching. No communal provisions or adoption are established.
  • Days 27–28: a replacement spindle is made. Two green dart candidates, a feather lot and an untested wooden atlatl remain distinct assets.

The earliest practical review should test food yield, bark-vessel reliability, heating-stone selection, fibre throughput and rawhide performance. Research Register records the evidence gaps; Civilisation Roadmap keeps later development tied to surplus and verified capabilities.

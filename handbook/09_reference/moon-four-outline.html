Moon Four Outline

Approved direction: the solitary first winter

The user approved the broad December direction and corrected the narrative arc on 23 October 2026: obtain food and clothing material independently; keep this first winter lonely; develop cooperation in spring. Earlier encounters, the worn wrap and the bounded Day 78 hunting/processing share remain written history. They do not become a continuing supply arrangement. The traveller chooses to establish a viable winter life of his own; the neighbours need not become hostile or disappear from the valley.

This revised sequence is a proposal for future pages. It replaces the 22 October shared-work/teaching plan. Flora and Fauna supplies the research frame. Continue natural connected prose, brief qualitative continuity and one principal technical question per foreground scene.

Calendar and starting position

December is the seasonal analogy: short days, cold rain, wind, frost and useful dry intervals, without imposing a precise modern weather record. Moon 4 has 29 days, continuous Days 89–117. Moon 4 Day 1 - A Warm Meal After the Rain and Reflection 07 - New Moon - Moon 4 Day 1 are already written. Full-moon review: Moon 4 Day 15 / Day 103. Closing review: after the Moon 5 Day 1 / Day 118 boundary entry.

The Western Overhang remains home. Rock is its roof, the ordinary hearth stays outside, and the small daub bay’s edge repair is still drying at the opening. There is one useful pot. The existing tan sleeveless body wrap, full extended fur cap and two thin low rabbit-skin foot covers remain the starting clothes; both arms and lower legs need better coverage. All existing skin is allocated, including the entire deer margin already used as Stone-Edge Guard.

One green half-stave, three green arrow blanks and a short nettle trial line exist. The ordinary wooden spear, stone-pointed hand spear, modified dart A with its wooden point/two vanes, and four-pronged fishing spear remain distinct. Bowed dart B is unfinished. No new skin, fat, successful hunt or dependable winter ration is granted by the outline.

Clothing: make a real material result the early turning point

Independent acquisition is plausible, but rabbits alone should not promise a complete winter outfit. Combine improved small-game trapping with an early serious solo ungulate-hunting opportunity. Red deer is the best-established local candidate; a smaller individual is more manageable than a large stag, while still demanding skill. A roe deer is an alternative only if an actual local discovery establishes it. No newly discovered dense rabbit colony or convenient predator kill substitutes for the work.

Plan towards an earned solo larger-animal result in the opening third, preceded by equipment checks, field observation and an approach that fits the ground. Use an existing working system, with the spear or atlatl chosen for the actual opportunity. Do not postpone warmth until an unmade bow succeeds. The result supplies one identifiable hide plus a bounded food-processing task; recovery and carrying matter as much as the cast. This is the intended story event, not a promise that real solo hunting is dependable. If the scene cannot credibly deliver it, adjust the quarry, preparation and pacing rather than inventing a free hide.

Begin skin preparation promptly and continue it alongside other work. Uploaded knowledge offers possible routes, but the actual hair-retaining dressing/softening method needs research for the available materials and wet winter conditions. Drying alone is not tanning. Do not assume salt, alum, an unallocated dressing-fat supply or new lessons from a neighbour. Keep the existing wrap in service while new material is worked.

Prioritise arms as well as legs: a simple removable shoulder-and-arm layer or fitted sleeves that close exposed upper-body gaps, plus overlapping leg coverings with secure ties. Improve the feet where sound, tougher material is available. Lay out the actual hide before cutting and decide these allocations together. If it cannot supply all three jobs, use explicitly earned additional small pelts for patches/linings or obtain further material. Mittens and a sleeping cover remain later uses only if their material exists. Do not promise a full hooded coat, trousers, boots and blanket from one skin. Update the dated character sheet as soon as the new outfit is actually completed.

Food in December

Stop routine nut gathering. The hazel crop is over in this seasonal model. Continue to inspect, protect and process the remaining autumn stores; a sound overlooked acorn is incidental, not another productive harvest. No generic roots, winter fruit, eggs or unidentified fungi arrive to fill a hidden deficit.

Use several independent food routes: a maintained small-game trap circuit selected by observed signs; the early ungulate result and later opportunities; and fishing when water conditions offer safe, clear access. Existing rabbit catches remain true, but northern archaeological scarcity argues against making rabbits the whole system (R56). Hares, birds or other small mammals require actual local observation rather than an assumed replacement abundance.

An ungulate result gives the pot a new task beyond acorn meals: fresh wet cooking, marrow recovery and, if the animal supplies suitable fatty tissue, a small fat-processing trial. Fat is not inferred from carcass size. Account for the hide and food as separate materials without returning to daily stock tables.

The larger food result also creates a genuine preservation and storage problem. Investigate a small cooked/dried trial with a suitably controlled process and a protected, ventilated storage arrangement away from dirty skin work and the bed. R07 remains a material constraint: smoke, frost or a dry surface does not establish safe long-term keeping. Select the actual method and explain its limits before counting retained food. Do not have one person eat an entire deer fresh on the day, or declare that one kill guarantees food through winter.

Traps deserve a real technical development

Treat trapping as a system linking animal behaviour, placement, trigger, line/anchor or closure, weather protection and inspection effort. Select a modest circuit near useful routes. A larger number of poorly placed devices is not automatically progress.

First inspect and improve the existing Figure-4 Deadfall Trap route: stable support, consistent release and protection from interference. Revisit the failed Snare Trap through actual fibre condition, chafing, attachment and trigger behaviour, with non-animal loading trials before a new field set. The broken Day 23 snare is not still operating.

Then choose one distinct small live-capture cage/covered drop-door design if observed quarry and weaving stock make it worthwhile. This adds a retaining closure and can preserve useful skin area, but trapping, retention and humane dispatch still need a sourced method and actual trial. It is a proposed design family, not a claim of an attested 40,000-BP local trap. A small netting trial can instead serve the same winter cordage/weaving theme if a cage has no worthwhile application; neither is a compulsory extra device.

The Woven Fishing Trap remains an existing apparatus whose placement, funnel, closure and retrieval can be reassessed. Avoid converting it into a river-spanning weir or assuming floodwater increases catches. One observed near-bank fish opportunity can use the dedicated spear as a variation. Modern winter salmonid behaviour is an analogy, not a scheduled salmon bonanza (R59, R61).

The bow offers a different possible handling/aiming arrangement, but its advantages must emerge through this person’s actual use. Wood condition can carry completion into the next moon; a calendar deadline is not a seasoning test (R50–R51). Never press bowed dart B into service merely to supply a spare.

Stronger cordage supports traps and later bow work; hide preparation creates warmth; food recovery and storage make longer craft projects possible. Keep shelter upkeep brief unless a real fault changes one of these decisions.

The timing can flex for material condition. Do not stretch every check, meal or unsuccessful set into a separate page, or impose a failure before each useful advance. Show warmth early enough to matter. The winter should feel lonely through work, silence and the absence of shared labour, rather than repeated speeches about isolation.

Fauna, future pages and the mission

Use Flora and Fauna for actual candidate choices. One distant megafaunal animal or credible sign can make this world feel unlike the modern countryside. Do not make exotic-animal kills the clothing plan, or march every researched species past camp. Keep the established valley map and terrain relationships.

Expand existing trap, clothing, hunting, cordage and food-processing pages as the new narrative demonstrates their limits and improvements. Add a separate skin-dressing route, garment variant, retention-trap design or preservation process only when each has distinct useful content. Keep future processes labelled as planned until actually performed. New substantive capability pages and the changed wardrobe need appropriate illustrations when the daily batches are commissioned; this planning revision creates no new equipment or artwork.

The mission is still present: a person must become warm, fed and able to reproduce useful processes before sustaining wider production. This winter can demonstrate independent competence and expose the costs of doing everything alone. Spring cooperation then becomes a consequential next stage. No new shared hunt, hide allocation, willing learner or group adoption is planned for December.

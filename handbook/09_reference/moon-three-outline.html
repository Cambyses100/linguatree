Moon Three Outline

Approved direction: establish a winter home

The user approved this course correction on 14 October 2026. Move from the old lean-to camp to a rocky overhang; make substantial warm clothing; secure seasonal food; develop useful cooking and hunting equipment. These foundations now lead the moon. Resource accounting becomes brief qualitative continuity, and the earlier retreat/cache programme is dropped.

Moon 1 and Moon 2 remain as written. Moon 3 Day 1, Moon 3 Day 1 - A Material Before a Vessel, remains intact. Its completed-day Reflection 05 - New Moon - Moon 3 Day 1 is revised at the user’s request to anticipate the winter priorities while retaining the Day 59 cutoff and historical snapshot. The correction begins with Moon 3 Day 2, continuous Day 60. The outline authorises the direction and intended progress; future pages still establish how discoveries, construction and exchanges occur.

The protagonist’s purpose remains technological. A dry sleeping chamber, a worked hide garment, seasonal storage, a sound spear joint and a cooking vessel are useful handbook subjects. Explain mechanisms and show consequential results. Ordinary gathering, fire care and water treatment can be glossed over when they do not change the project.

Calendar and seasonal pressure

Moon 3 is late autumn, roughly November in our planning analogy, about two months after the early-autumn arrival. No exact Gregorian arrival date or reconstructed local weather series is imposed. Shorter useful daylight, colder mornings, wet windy intervals and occasional frost make winter preparations urgent. Include dry intervals for building, skin work and firing rather than an uninterrupted succession of storms.

The Lunar Calendar model gives Moon 3 thirty days, continuous Days 59–88. The full-moon reflection is Moon 3 Day 16 / continuous Day 74. The next new-moon review is Moon 4 Day 1 / continuous Day 89, after that boundary day’s entry. Future reflections describe the completed state in prose, without daily resource-balance tables.

Starting point, without an inventory recital

He has working stone edges, simple woodworking tools, a wooden spear used previously for fishing, an experimental atlatl and dart, a fish trap, two ignition sets and two bark vessels. His shelter has improved but remains a poor winter commitment. The grass-panel outfit and little pelts provide limited coverage. He has two fired clay comparison pieces, but no accepted ceramic vessel.

Food access and material availability still constrain work. Explain a significant shortage, needed hide, new shaft or long drying process where it changes a decision. Do not record routine meals, fuel bundles, cord metres, water volumes and hourly balances every day. Preserve obvious continuity: an exchanged hide has a source, a consumed catch does not return, and a single skin cannot furnish an entire wardrobe without regard to its area.

The rocky-overhang home

The move is a settled narrative direction, not another debate about remaining at the lean-to. Introduce a reachable overhang on the same side of the main river as the established routes, with usable water access and room for ordinary food/fuel work. Inspect rock stability, drips, runoff, wind, animal signs and evidence of other occupants. Its exact position is established in the discovery entry and then added to Valley Map and a new place note. The existing map does not already contain this site.

Choose a dry, useful overhang rather than a deep cave. Rock supplies much of the roof and rear protection. Build the missing weather-facing enclosure: a sound low frame and woven/brush screen, overlapping weather cover, a manageable entrance and thick clean dry bedding on an appropriately separated resting surface. Keep natural drainage outside the occupied space and allow moisture to escape.

Use clay-rich earth mixed with plant fibre selectively on supported front or lower wall panels to reduce draughts. First inspect a small panel as it dries. The earth is not a blanket coating on the old roof or a substitute for bedding. Wet earth adds loading and moisture; sheltering the panel from direct rain matters. R40 informs the moisture assessment, not a prehistoric construction specification.

Move usable tools, bedding and protected supplies to the new home once basic occupation is possible. Abandon the lean-to as the principal camp; record the move once. The old site can remain an abandoned location in the atlas, without being maintained as a second dwelling or defensive retreat. Do not turn the old fallback bench into this overhang or quietly build a cache there.

Re-establish clean craft, food, water, animal-processing, waste and firing relationships at the new site. Keep the ordinary hearth in the open outside the enclosed sleeping area. An overhang alone does not prove adequate exhaust; an indoor fire would require a separate design decision. The new sleeping screen, bedding and garments remain combustible.

A coherent winter outfit

Make larger animal-skin coverage an early practical objective. The first substantial garment should be an overlapping shoulder/body wrap, then a roughly fitted belted garment if the skin area and flexibility permit. Follow with head/neck coverage, leg wraps and enclosed foot coverings with renewable dry plant lining. Hand protection can follow from available pieces. A warm sleeping covering and the bed work together.

The earliest useful wrap should appear in the first phase rather than waiting until the latter half of the moon. Obtain one or more larger usable skins through accepted work/exchange or a realistically earned share of animal processing. Show who supplies the skin, its existing condition and what work or object is accepted in return. An already worked worn skin can give immediate coverage while fresh material is being prepared. Small catches alone do not instantly yield a large coat.

Local hide-working knowledge is a practical resource. The traveller contributes ordinary useful work and learns material judgements from competent people. Work new skins over several sessions; drying alone is not durable tanning. Research the chosen preservation/conditioning route when drafting that process. R28, R30 and R39 provide context, not a reconstructed local garment pattern.

The old grass panels and bark layers become temporary underlayers, spare material or items set aside as warmer coverage takes over. Describe that transition once. Clothing should look like functional coverage rather than an accumulation of ornaments. Allocate actual skin area between the main garment, hood/cap, foot pieces and other coverings. Update the character sheet after the new daily pages establish the wardrobe, so later images retain it.

Food for this month and the colder season

Use a combination of seasonal gathered food, ongoing animal food and protected stores, supported by local knowledge and accepted cooperation. The goal is credible access and useful preparation, not a guaranteed stock of a specified weight.

  • While sound nuts remain accessible, gather hazelnuts and acorns in worthwhile batches and create protected, ventilated storage off damp ground. Establish which patches he can use, declining returns and any useful local guidance.
  • Store suitable untreated acorns as processing stock and prepare meals as needed through the already established crushing/leaching/cooking route. Wet meal remains perishable. A sound basket, pounding surface, water access and workable batch size are important technologies.
  • Keep productive small-game traps in ordinary use, compressing uneventful checks. A catch becomes foreground when it changes food access, hide work or the next technical choice.
  • Alternate the fish trap with renewed spear fishing at appropriate clear, shallow accessible margins. His earlier spear-fishing trials used the ordinary wooden spear. The multi-pronged design has not yet been built; introduce it as a possible new fishing tool if the ordinary spear exposes a useful improvement.
  • Work towards an accepted role in local gathering, processing or hunting. Shared work can produce an explicitly observed share of food or skin without making the group responsible for feeding him. R41 supports the regional relevance of larger animal prey; it does not promise a catch or dictate this group’s arrangements.
  • Where surplus genuinely exists, make drying/preservation a deliberate food-technology project, researched for the method and conditions. Smoke, cold weather or a dry-looking surface does not alone establish durable meat stores (R07).

Animal food includes usable fat and marrow where actually available; greens, occasional fish and a few rabbits do not automatically fund months of cold-weather work. Known hazel and oak patches remain fictional local resource assumptions, not an inexhaustible winter harvest. If a source is poor, change the effort or cooperation rather than supplying an identical daily return.

Keep food away from dirty skin work, waste and damp. Inspect stores and acknowledge a consequential loss if it occurs, without logging every portion. Describe whether the supply is improving, strained or supporting a longer project. Quantities are useful only where they explain capacity, a significant processing batch or a material choice.

Spear, atlatl and the route towards a bow

Carry and maintain a useful spear, develop the atlatl for hunting, and keep relations with the nearby group workable. Defensive awareness belongs in ordinary behaviour. It does not require an escape-route chapter, a supplied fallback shelter or a sequence of warnings.

The existing Fire-Hardened Spear page covers the wooden spear. Shape and maintain a sound point; any fire-treatment comparison should remain a small material question. R05 found trade-offs under its tested treatment, so do not present charring as an automatic strength upgrade. A stone-pointed hafted spear is a useful next project: suitable shaft, shaped point, seating geometry and binding, followed by an appropriate joint/impact trial. Local competence can inform the work, with any borrowed or transferred component described clearly. The ordinary single-point wooden spear, also used for fishing, need not be dismantled to create every new weapon. It is distinct from the still-unmade Multi-Pronged Fishing Spear.

Continue the Atlatl through a suitable dart, retention/release, consistency and a hunting configuration. Improve real limiting parts instead of repeating broad target practice daily. Fletching and a point are useful when the shaft and joints justify them. Hunting adds approach, opportunity, recovery and processing; a good target result is not all of those skills.

Begin thinking and preparing for a bow during the latter part of the moon. Select actually available wood with useful grain and condition, prepare a stave, and explore a low-stretch string and suitable arrow stock. Tillering means controlling where the limbs bend; string fit, arrow straightness and fletching are separate requirements. His bow-drill bow is not an existing hunting bow.

A modest practice prototype is possible if material condition and the rest of the winter work permit. A finished hunting bow is not a required Day 30 outcome. If the stave needs further seasoning, the preparation still gives Moon 4 a clear project. Do not invent ideal yew, a ready bowstring or a finished arrow set to meet a deadline. The traveller’s remembered design is an authored anticipatory experiment, not a claim that the local group lacks bows.

Pottery with a useful winter purpose

Retain the successful comparison pieces as background information and shape one modest vessel with an approximately even section. Give it protected drying room at the new home while food, clothing and weapons progress. Fire on suitable separate ground when the body and weather allow, cool before handling, then establish water containment and an appropriate heating/cooking role.

The point is a container that makes meals or water treatment more manageable. Make the preparation, firing and first use clear without turning each minor inspection into a separate dramatic hurdle. Preserve the bark route while the ceramic route proves useful. Direct-fire cooking, food-contact suitability and repeated heating need attention appropriate to the chosen body and role; no universal safe firing recipe is implied.

A second matched vessel is worthwhile once the first route is useful. A large kiln, metallurgy programme or procession of ceremonial prototypes would distract from the month’s purpose.

Relations and the wider purpose

Keep on the right side of the group through respectful access, ordinary useful contributions, reciprocal learning and repeated familiar contact. Let relationships develop through the winter work. They need not culminate in a formal apprenticeship, leadership role or compulsory independent-copying test this moon.

Knowledge sharing remains important to Civilisation Roadmap, but warm working people and an adequately provisioned home are the immediate foundation. A willingly shared operation can appear when it is useful rather than becoming another scheduled performance. No exotic invention is an entitlement to skins, food or hospitality.

Lighter continuity from Day 60

Use the compact narrative template in Page Templates. Keep dates, capability links and only consequential changes: the camp move, a larger skin acquired, a new garment or weapon, a significant exchange, a serious shortage or an item actually lost. Routine food, fuel, water and sleep do not need numeric daily entries or inventory balances.

Reflections assess what now works, what still needs work and the next priorities in prose. Exact modelling through Day 59 stays as a historical record; it does not become a current inventory for the new period. Physical plausibility and major object/material continuity remain editorial responsibilities rather than a spreadsheet maintained throughout the story.

Intended end state

Aim for occupation of the rocky-overhang home, substantially warmer practical clothing, seasonal food stores plus ongoing food access, an improved spear and atlatl, renewed spear-fishing use and a ceramic vessel with an earned useful role. Bow-and-arrow preparation supplies the next technical direction.

The old lean-to is abandoned as the home, the fallback/cache programme is discontinued, and regular exact-resource reporting ends. None of these future achievements is entered into the actual daily history until its page is written. This revision creates no new daily prose, pictures, mapped discoveries or website publication.

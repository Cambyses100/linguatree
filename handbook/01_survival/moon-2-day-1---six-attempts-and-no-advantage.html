Moon 2 Day 1 - Six Attempts and No Advantage

Make a projectile for a question

At the craft spot you finish the less distorted shaft into a blunt atlatl dart. Its front is rounded; its rear receives a conservative seat for the actual wooden hook. You leave the feathers in their dry lot. Adding them now would introduce another alignment and attachment problem before you know whether the bare pair releases.

The base route on Atlatl Dart has produced an experimental object. It is not ready for hunting, and the wood is still green. You accept it only for modest attempts after rechecking its grain, gentle flex and slow engagement. A changing specimen will need checking again tomorrow.

A place to stop a miss

At the selected ground you remove a few loose obstructions and mark a short launch line with collected sticks. You lay the reference staff repeatedly along the same accessible line to the broad earth bank. The count is a local comparison; the slope and imperfect placements make an exact distance claim pointless.

The flight area is clear, but it will not stay clear by virtue of a name. You look towards the contact route, check the bank’s sides, and set the ordinary spear outside the casting swing where you can reach it. There is no target hide or cord stretched across a path.

The first comparison

You make three modest hand casts with the same blunt dart, retrieving and inspecting it between attempts. They reach different places. Familiarity with a wooden spear does not make this thinner projectile feel identical.

The three thrower attempts are less reassuring. On the first, the rear leaves the hook before the useful part of the movement and the dart drops short. The second releases but lands well to one side of your intended patch. The third reaches the bank crookedly. You inspect the end after each attempt and find no spreading split, but that is a reason to retain the experiment, not to increase effort.

Projectile Testing keeps these events separate. You scratch six attempt marks and distinguish the hand-cast group from the thrower group. You do not turn the best landing into a claimed range or velocity. There is too little evidence, and your movement is too inconsistent, to judge an advantage.

Atlatl Throwing asks you to coordinate something your remembered explanation can describe more easily than your body can repeat. You stop before fatigue makes every subsequent result less useful. The unused feather lot and second shaft stay at camp.

End of day

The model records 2.34 kg of kernels and 20 kg of dry fuel. One green blunt dart has replaced candidate A; candidate B remains a blank. The atlatl has been launched, but no useful hunting performance or stable grouping is established.

Estimated active work is 8.5 hours; sleep allowance is eight hours. Survival Ledger records the conversion rather than counting both shaft and finished dart.

Lunar reflection

After the first erratic casts, pause for Reflection 03 - New Moon - Moon 2 Day 1. This new-moon review weighs supply, exposure and intentions before another week of work.

Next decision

The first failures do not justify another public pitch. Start the fibre supply that bedding and clothing will require, and return to short practice when essential work permits.
